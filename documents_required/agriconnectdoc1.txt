2. Executive Summary
AgriConnect is a web platform for agriculture with two layers. The first is a labour marketplace: farmers post jobs, nearby workers apply, a transparent matching engine ranks candidates with explanations, bookings are tracked and both sides rate each other. The second layer is farm intelligence: a profit simulator, crop guidance, crop-disease detection from photos, a question-answering assistant grounded in trusted documents, and market price information.
Where the project stands: the marketplace backend is built in Python and FastAPI, runs locally, and passes 9 automated tests covering the full flow from registration to ratings. The remaining work is the user interface, hardening, the profit simulator, one or two carefully evaluated AI modules, cloud deployment and user validation.
Why it is a strong final-year project: it solves a real problem, has a working core, uses a deliberately simple baseline that ML must beat, and treats evaluation, safety and honesty as part of the engineering rather than as afterthoughts.
3. Vision, Problem and Objectives
3.1 Vision
A trusted digital bridge between farmers and workers that also helps farmers make better decisions, built so that people with a basic phone and limited literacy can use it.
3.2 Problem statement
•	Farmers often find labour through personal contacts, at the last moment, with little information about reliability.
•	Workers lack visibility of nearby work, clear wages and dates, and have no portable record of their reputation.
•	Small farmers have limited access to timely, trustworthy advice on crop choice, disease and selling decisions.

4. Scope
4.1 In scope
•	Accounts and roles for farmers and workers; farms; worker profiles; jobs; applications; bookings; reviews; rule-based matching.
•	Responsive web front end (PWA) with local-language support and offline basics.
•	Farmer profit simulator with scenarios and selling-option comparison.
•	At least one fully evaluated AI module (crop-disease detection or RAG), with the other as stretch.
•	Notifications (in-app first, then WhatsApp or SMS).
•	Cloud deployment, CI/CD, monitoring, documentation and a final report.
4.2 Out of scope (first release)
•	Payments, wallets and payroll; legal contracts and insurance.
•	Fully autonomous AI actions; any guarantee of yield, price or diagnosis.
•	Native mobile apps (the PWA covers mobile for now).
•	Large-scale pricing forecasts and drone or satellite analysis.
4.3 Priority approach
Features are ranked with MoSCoW. Must: marketplace, auth, tests, front end, one evaluated AI module. Should: profit simulator, notifications, local language, offline. Could: agent, market forecasts, group applications, ML-based matching. If time is short the Could items are dropped first.
4.4 Assumptions and constraints
•	One developer, one academic year, student cloud credits.
•	No real matching data at launch; public datasets for ML; curated documents for RAG.
•	Pilot users are reachable for interviews and testing.

6.1 Farmer profit simulator: why it matters
The simulator turns farm economics into a transparent calculation the farmer can adjust: revenue, total cost, net profit, profit per acre, break-even price and yield, low/base/high scenarios, and a comparison of selling options after transport, commission, storage and shrinkage. It needs no training data, can be tested exactly, and links to the marketplace because labour cost can be filled from the wages actually posted on AgriConnect. It is a good early AI-adjacent feature because it delivers value before any model is trained.
7. Solution Summary and Decision Log
7.1 Architecture in brief
A modular monolith: React front end, FastAPI REST API with JWT and role checks, SQLAlchemy over PostgreSQL (SQLite in development), internal modules for matching and later ML/RAG/simulator, Docker for packaging and Azure as the cloud target. Full detail is in the Technical Design Document.

Frontend	React + TypeScript, Vite, React Query; PWA
AI	PyTorch or scikit-learn; pgvector for retrieval; LLM through an adapter
DevOps	Docker, GitHub Actions, Azure (Container Apps, PostgreSQL, Blob Storage, Key Vault, Monitor)

16. Legal, Ethical and Safety Considerations
•	Advice disclaimer: crop, disease and profit outputs are decision support, not professional or guaranteed advice; users are directed to local agricultural officers for important decisions.
•	AI limits: show confidence, cite sources, refuse unsupported answers, and never let the agent take consequential actions without explicit confirmation.
•	Privacy: minimal data, secure storage, deletion on request, location shown only where needed; review India's DPDP Act 2023 before real-user use.
•	Fairness: matching must not use caste, religion, gender or similar attributes; reputation should be explainable and contestable.
•	Labour considerations: show wages clearly; no payments in the first release; encourage fair pay information (average local wage) without enforcing prices.
•	Data and content licences: use only datasets, documents and prices whose licences permit the use; cite sources in the report.

18. Demo Script and Viva Preparation
18.1 Five-minute demo script
1.	Problem in one sentence (30 seconds), with a quote from a farmer interview if available.
2.	Farmer logs in, shows the farm, posts a groundnut harvesting job.
3.	Worker logs in on a phone, sees the job under nearby jobs with distance and wage, and applies.
4.	Farmer opens ranked matches and points out the reasons behind the top score; accepts the worker.
5.	Farmer marks the work complete; both sides rate each other; the worker's average rating updates.
6.	Farmer opens the profit simulator, compares two selling options and shows break-even price.
7.	Show one AI feature: upload a leaf photo, see label, confidence and cited guidance.
8.	Close with test results, evaluation numbers and what is planned next.
18.2 Likely viva questions and answers
Question	Suggested answer
Why did you start with a rule-based matcher?	There is no outcome data yet, rules are transparent and explainable, and they provide a baseline that any ML model must beat.
Why Python and FastAPI?	ML, RAG and agent components are Python-native, so one language keeps the system simple; FastAPI gives validation and API docs automatically. The original plan was Java; the architecture did not change.
How do you secure the API?	bcrypt password hashing, signed JWTs, role checks on routes, ownership checks on every job-related action, input validation, restricted CORS; all covered by tests. Rate limiting and refresh tokens are planned.
How is the match score computed?	Weighted sum: distance 40%, availability 20%, skill 20%, experience 10%, reputation 10%, each scaled 0 to 1; unrated workers get a neutral reputation; reasons are returned with each result.
How do you prevent two farmers or workers interfering?	Every protected action checks the user's role and ownership; for example only the farm owner can see applicants or accept a worker; tests cover these cases.
What stops duplicate applications or reviews?	Application logic checks plus database uniqueness constraints on (job, worker) and (booking, reviewer).
How will you evaluate the disease model honestly?	Fixed split, per-class metrics and a confusion matrix, plus a separate test on real field photos to show the gap from dataset accuracy.
How do you stop the AI giving harmful advice?	RAG with citations, refusal when sources are insufficient, confidence thresholds, disclaimers, expert review of content, and confirmation before any action.
What does the profit simulator do and why is it reliable?	Deterministic formulas for revenue, cost, profit and break-even with scenario ranges; every formula is unit-tested; results are labelled as estimates.
How would it scale?	The API is stateless so it can run as several replicas; add pagination, geospatial indexes, caching, background jobs for ML; move to managed PostgreSQL.
What are the limitations?	No payments, straight-line distance, no migrations yet, no refresh tokens, limited real data; all are listed with planned fixes.
What is novel?	Explainable labour matching tied to reputation, combined with farm decision support (profit simulation, cited advice) in one low-literacy-friendly platform, with honest evaluation of each AI part.
What would you do with more time?	Payments with escrow, route-based distance, ML ranking from real outcomes, more languages, offline-first sync, and expert-reviewed knowledge base.
